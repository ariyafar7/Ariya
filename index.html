Run-through notes

How people find it

This is a concept Doodle, not an official one. On World IBS Day (19 April), a pixel Michael tile on the search homepage opens the game. Searches about IBS, gut pain or stress also show it beside the results. On a phone it opens full-screen. One tap closes it to a small "Resume" pill.

Story

The game opens outside the body. Michael stands beside a person's silhouette, with a faint outline of their gut showing. Michael introduces himself, and the player chooses who to follow:

  • Sam: a first-year uni student juggling assignments, exams, deadlines and a social life.
  • Alex: a new graduate starting a first full-time job, with new responsibilities, expectations and deadlines.

The scene then zooms into the person's mouth, and the dive begins. Every scenario, the three decisions at the end and the stress bubbles change to match the chosen person.

Flow (tap Jump to preview any screen)

    Interaction types

    • Scenario multiple choice: mouth, small intestine, ileum, colon
    • True/false quick-fire: oesophagus
    • Put in order: the stress chain in the stomach
    • Tap the scene: find the alarm cell next to the nerve (with a true/false fallback)
    • Choose the path (rectum): 3 decisions that move a stress meter and a gut meter
    • Poo chart (exit): tap the 7 poo types of the Bristol Stool Form Scale, then a question on how IBS types show up in poo. A cute pixel poo waves goodbye.
    • Swipe to sort: build the gut kit, then pick one thing to try this week

    Mini-games every three questions:

    • After stop 3, pop the stress bubbles: bubbles labelled with the person's stressors float down. Each pop reveals one step of the stress chain, from the brain to the gut bacteria. A breathing break follows.
    • After stop 6, meet another microbe: Michael meets Bifi, his purple twin, a fibre-loving gut bacterium (Bifidobacterium). The player picks questions, and Bifi explains how stress changes the microbes' world, what she needs to thrive, and how microbes talk back to the brain. Bifi then joins the colony.

    Everything works by tapping, so there's no typing on a phone. Swiping also works with the buttons or the arrow keys.

    Design decisions

    • Practical first. Each stop starts with something the player recognises and ends with one tip. Scientific terms are kept for "Read the research".
    • Read more, not ratings. Each stop has an optional "Read more" section: a short plain-language summary plus the papers behind it as further reading. It stays closed unless the player opens it.
    • Story first. The mini-games and Bifi's conversation carry the science through characters instead of extra quiz questions.
    • IBS-specific advice. Diet tips follow the IBS guideline (soluble fibre added slowly, a dietitian before strict diets), not general "gut health" advice.
    • No timer, and never punishing. Wrong answers still move Michael down, and every answer explains itself.
    • A breathing break halfway models a stress-calming tool instead of only describing it.
    • Sound is off by default because it's on a search page.

    Fact-check before final (see EVIDENCE.md)

    • Serotonin "about 9 in 10 made in the gut": confirm Gershon & Tack 2007 and add its link.
    • Stress and dry mouth: add a source.
    • "Regular meals" as first-line advice, and the "see a doctor" list: confirm against BSG 2021 or a local guideline.
    • Check every quoted number against the full paper.

    Core readings

    • Vicario M, et al. Chronic psychosocial stress … in the rat intestine and IBS-like gut dysfunction. Psychoneuroendocrinology 2012;37:65–77.
