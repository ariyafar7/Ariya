Run-through notes

How people find it

On World IBS Day (19 April), a search for terms like "IBS", "stomach pain stress" or "gut health" opens this card beside the results. On a phone it slides up as a bottom sheet. One tap starts the game, and one tap closes it to a small "Resume" pill.

Flow (tap Jump to preview any screen)

    What the player sees on the way down

    • Mouth: rows of pixel teeth on both walls, plus a few oral bacteria.
    • Oesophagus: a narrow tube with muscle rings.
    • Stomach: folded walls and a bubbling pool of yellow stomach acid (about pH 2) that Bifi swims through.
    • Duodenum: orange-tinted walls with yellow bile dripping in.
    • Jejunum and ileum: finger-like villi on the walls, with more and more resident microbes.
    • Colon: pouched walls and a crowded, mixed community: rods, cocci clusters, chains, spiky and ciliated microbes.

    Design decisions

    • 8-bit look. Retro pixel art feels like a game, not a health warning, and stays light enough for a pop-up.
    • No timer. The reference game uses a countdown. Our audience is already under exam and job-hunt pressure, so the dive is self-paced.
    • Wrong answers still move you down. Every answer explains itself, so nobody gets stuck or feels tested.
    • The colony grows. Each correct answer adds a different microbe friend. A bigger, more varied community stands for a healthier microbiota.
    • Learn more is optional. The question stays at plain-language level. Mechanism detail and sources sit behind "Learn more".
    • A breathing break at halfway models one stress-calming tool instead of only describing it.
    • Real units. The depth readout uses metres along an adult gut (about 8.9 m in this draft).
    • Sound is off by default, since the game pops up on a search page.
    • Original sprites. The microbe art is drawn for this project in the style of the team's reference image, not copied from it.

    Fact-check before final

    • "About 90% of the body's serotonin is made in the gut" is widely cited but is not in our two core readings. Add a supporting source.
    • "Some bacteria can sense stress hormones directly" (colon stop, Learn more) comes from microbial endocrinology research, not our two readings. Add a source or cut it.
    • Stomach acid "about pH 2", teeth grinding linked to stress, and GI tract lengths are general knowledge. Add a source for each.
    • "When to see a doctor" red flags follow standard IBS guidance. Check them against a current local guideline before publishing.
    • The crowding study was in rats. The game says so, and it should stay that way in later edits.
